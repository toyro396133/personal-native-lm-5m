# Blind Follow-up Hypothesis Snapshot

Frozen before any follow-up benchmark execution.

## Scope
This snapshot preserves the interpretations reached from the first blind analysis of `four-component-blind-dataset-47`. It intentionally does not identify the real meanings of A/B/C/D or V1–V7.

## Component hypotheses

### A
- Observed: directionally consistent early in depth; intervention effect tends to strengthen later; learned-value usefulness is not identical to effect magnitude.
- Frozen interpretation: A is a dynamic control/reference axis or routing-like signal. Its existence and magnitude can matter even when the exact learned value does not.
- Confidence: medium-high for “distinct dynamic axis”; medium for “reference/control”.
- Falsifier: norm- and angle-matched perturbations show no direction-specific downstream effect, or effects are equally explained by generic magnitude/noise.

### B
- Observed: highly stable and decodable across conditions, including V1 where measured A effects are absent.
- Frozen interpretation: B is a relatively stable identity/content-like anchor, not dependent on the measured A mechanism for existence.
- Confidence: high for stability; medium for “anchor”.
- Falsifier: selective B perturbation fails to alter B-related downstream behavior, while perturbing another component changes B more strongly and specifically.

### C
- Observed: geometry is naturally measured relative to B; its margin to B has a characteristic layer trajectory and is often stronger internally than at final.
- Frozen interpretation: C is a relative/contextual component whose functional expression depends partly on relation to B.
- Confidence: medium.
- Falsifier: C-specific perturbation has effects independent of B state and B-substitution never changes C-related behavior beyond generic perturbation size.

### D
- Observed: B under D is often harder than B under C; current evidence is insufficient to know whether D is causal, competing, or merely a harder condition.
- Frozen interpretation: D is a challenging/competing variable or context dimension that interferes more broadly with stable B readout.
- Confidence: low-medium.
- Falsifier: matched D interventions are no more disruptive/selective than C, or apparent D difficulty vanishes under new prompt/context banks.

## Relationship hypotheses
1. B is the most stable of the four observed factors.
2. A and B are two different kinds of “anchors”: B stable/content-like; A dynamic/reference/control-like.
3. A is not necessary for B to exist, but may modulate how other relations are expressed.
4. C is more relational to B than D is.
5. D may reflect broader competition/entanglement rather than a clean relative relation.
6. Effect strength, directional consistency, and learned-value specificity are distinct quantities and must remain analytically separate.

## Depth hypothesis
Frozen sequence: early A direction/structure -> temporary geometric compression/bottleneck near layer 2 -> recovery/reorganization around layers 3–4 -> strongest A-like functional effect around layers 5–6 -> partial geometric loss at final while some task accessibility remains.

Falsifier: causal patching shows the purported early/middle stages are epiphenomenal, with only final or a different layer band carrying downstream leverage.

## Variant-family hypotheses
- V1: structurally distinct control-like family; B remains strong without measured A effect.
- V2–V3: relatively A-sensitive family, especially under sign-like perturbation.
- V4: A-sensitive but with more positive learned-A specificity for C-like readouts.
- V5: strong A effect/amplification but weak or negative learned-value specificity for C-like readouts; possible shortcut/reference mismatch.
- V6–V7: comparatively robust B under A interventions; candidate robust family.

These families are provisional and must be revised only after the preregistered follow-up benchmarks are complete.
